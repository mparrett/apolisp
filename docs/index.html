apolisp

A small Lisp in the Clojure dialect, with its own VM, in Rust. One user, no compatibility contract, no stability promise. These twelve write-ups are not about the language — they are about the loop built to check it, and about the six or seven times that loop reported a pass it had not earned.

10 milestones/6,845 lines/57 decisions/12 write-ups

The project has an unusual amount of machinery for its size: an append-only decision log, a golden-file corpus, a mutation rung, a subtraction build, a hard line budget, and a habit of writing down what a change is expected to do before doing it. Ten milestones in, the interesting finding is not that the machinery worked. It is how many of these mechanisms turned out to have a blind spot exactly where they were most trusted — and that in almost every case the thing that found it was a person deliberately trying to break something that was already green.

Read in any order. If you want one page: Review reads the assertion, nothing reads the subject has the idea the others keep rediscovering. If you want the story in order, start with chapter one.

The thread

The same defect, under five names

Read together, most of these pages are one finding arriving in different costume. A check is a claim about a pair — an assertion, and the subject it was applied to. Review reads the assertion. Nothing reads the subject, and the subject is the half that drifts.

  • A property whose assertions were correct, applied to bytecode the VM never runs.
  • A mutation run reporting a survivor, where the substitution never applied — and a second where it applied and the optimizer deleted it before it reached the binary.
  • A soak asserting nothing leaked, over 150,000 injected leaks that were never allocated.
  • A budget passing while a fifth of it vanished, a layer went unbudgeted for four milestones, and an exclusion turned into a way to move lines out by moving files.
  • A benchmark measuring the one dominant quadratic, with every other cost in the program invisible until that one was gone.

The flattering reading is the wrong one, and from outside the two are identical.

The response is the same in every case, and never a better assertion. It is to make the subject visible — print what the check observed, print what the budget skipped, print the counters and not just the verdict — and then have someone read it. The assertions are the backstop. The printout is the instrument.

On the numbers here, and the freeze

Counts on this page are a snapshot taken at the freeze, 2026-08-01, not a live inventory. The project’s own rule, arrived at the hard way, is that an inventory stated in prose is a correction you have already scheduled — so the files are the count, and this page is a description rather than something anything defers to.

apolisp is a personal project with one user, no third-party users, and no stability promise — the language may break on a Tuesday and be rebuilt on Wednesday. The core is one Rust file plus a prelude written in the language, with host adapters outside the line budget and the exclusion printed on every run. Everything on these pages is quoted or counted from the repository: docs/ADR.md for decisions, docs/notes/ for the working notes each write-up is built from, and the tests themselves for anything with a number in it.

Frozen 2026-08-01. These write-ups describe the initial development of apolisp — milestones 1 to 10 and the sessions that followed — and are not maintained. Every count on this page is true as of that date. A later phase would be a new series rather than an edit to this one.
